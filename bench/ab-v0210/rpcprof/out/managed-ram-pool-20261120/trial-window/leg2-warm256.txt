The sea is salty mainly because water slowly dissolves minerals from rocks and carries them into the ocean. The first process that adds salt is weathering: rain and groundwater break down land rocks, releasing dissolved ions such as sodium, chloride, calcium, and sulfate, which rivers transport to the sea. The second process is hydrothermal circulation: seawater seeps into cracks in the ocean floor, is heated by magma, reacts with hot basalt, and returns to the ocean carrying dissolved metals and other salts.
