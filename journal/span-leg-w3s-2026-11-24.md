## Why

## engine_breakdown.py

Reads all four line families; a log with none of them prints the counts and a hint naming
`STRATA_ENGINE_SPANS=1` (that hint was unreachable before - the empty-log `return` came first;
fixed). For a real span leg, by section:

| Section | Field that answers "where does the port lose time" |
|---|---|
| chunk walls | `walk` vs `intake` vs `tail` share of `chunk_us` - is the loss inside the layer loop or around it |
| layer spans | per-segment share of `layer_us`, claiming vs non-claiming layers (the split's 32..47 are the non-claiming half; their `post_us` is the whole remaining layer) |
| router decomposition | `barrier` is the compute-stream sync - the prime suspect for a scheduling/queue difference |
| pass/run + `--wall` | how much of the leg wall the run covers, and what sits outside it |

Read as **shares within each arm**, compared segment by segment A vs B. It is a localisation, not
a rate.

## Instrumented tok/s is not a speed claim

The gate reads two clocks per walked layer and writes one line per layer per chunk. A W3s tok/s is
an instrumented rate: never comparable to an uninstrumented leg, and its A-vs-B ratio is **not**
the size of the prefill difference (the rate it prints is context only). Nothing from this window
is poolable with W1/W1d/W1f/W2/W3/W3p.
